GT (Grand Touring) car racing is a globally popular form of motorsport featuring high-performance vehicles that are based on production sports cars and built for speed, performance, and endurance. Unlike purpose-built open-wheel or prototype race cars, GT cars maintain a recognizable link to the exotic machines found on public roads, such as the Aston Martin Vantage, Ferrari 296, Lamborghini Huracán, Mercedes-AMG GT, and Porsche 911. This connection to road-going supercars is a major part of their broad appeal, combining the glamour of luxury brands with the raw excitement of circuit racing.

Today, GT racing is primarily organized around a clear class structure managed by the FIA (Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile) and the SRO Motorsports Group, designed to ensure close competition through a Balance of Performance (BoP) system. GT3: This is the most prevalent and professional class globally. GT3 cars are highly modified versions of their road-going counterparts, featuring advanced aerodynamics, powerful engines (around 500-600 horsepower), and sophisticated electronics like adjustable ABS and traction control. They are the top tier in many series, including the GT World Challenge Europe and America, and since 2024, the top GT class at the 24 Hours of Le Mans (LMGT3). GT4: Serving as a more accessible entry point, GT4 cars are closer to production models with fewer modifications and less aerodynamic grip than GT3 cars. This category is ideal for amateur drivers and provides a robust platform for national and regional series, such as the British GT Championship and GT4 European Series. Other Classes: Historically, there have been more extreme classes like GT1 and GT2, which at times blurred the line with sports prototypes. Japan's Super GT series uses unique GT500 and GT300 classes, with GT500 cars being high-downforce, prototype-like machines.

LMP 4

An "LMP 4 car" likely refers to a Le Mans Prototype (LMP) in general, or could be a misremembered reference to the specific McLaren MP4/4 Formula 1 car or Cadillac Northstar LMP, which were a type of prototype race car. LMP cars are high-tech prototypes designed for endurance racing, while the McLaren MP4/4 was a highly successful Formula 1 car. The term "LMP" can also refer to the current LMP2 class rules for endurance racing cars, which have specific technical regulations.

LMP 3

An LMP3 car is an entry-level prototype sports racing car, designed for cost-effective endurance racing and serving as a stepping stone to higher-tier prototype classes. Key features include a spec engine (a twin-turbo V6 for the 2025 generation), a minimum weight, and regulations that aim to balance performance with affordability for developing drivers and teams.

LMP 2

An LMP2 is a closed-cockpit endurance racing car designed for independent teams, known for being the second-fastest prototype class after Hypercar. Key characteristics include a standardized, cost-controlled spec package with a Gibson V8 engine, a "pro-am" driver structure requiring at least one professional and one "gentleman" driver, and a highly competitive field where setup and strategy play a huge role

GT 3

A GT3 car is a high-performance, road-legal race car homologated for the FIA's GT3 class, which serves as a high-level category for racing series like the 24 Hours of Le Mans. These cars, such as the Porsche 911 GT3 and BMW M4 GT3, are built with an emphasis on track performance, featuring powerful naturally aspirated or turbocharged engines, advanced aerodynamics, and a focus on driver control, while still meeting street-legal requirements.

GT 4

A "GT4 car" can refer to two things: a specific high-performance model like the Porsche 718 Cayman GT4, or a category of race car that includes vehicles like the Ford Mustang GT4. The Porsche 718 Cayman GT4 is a street-legal sports car with a flat-6 engine, while a GT4 race car is a purpose-built vehicle for the FIA GT4 racing series, designed for amateur and young drivers.
